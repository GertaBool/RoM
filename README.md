# Rise of Mankind research and source fixes

Source-based assessment of [aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind on GitLab](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind), pinned to revision `1ab4f99a76adb530f86b278390ab8139ef41cc96`.

New: patch 0019, landed as [!44](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/44), [fixes three war AI bugs](fixes/README.md#war-ai-fixes-retraining-disband-collateral-counted-only-when-it-does-damage). City units were disbanded for retraining exactly when enemies were near. Lead From Behind let any unit with collateral attack first, even an archer whose collateral does no damage. Against an expert, the chance of losing a reachable city next turn falls from 13.4 % to 8.9 %, and turns are 22 % faster. Before that, patch 0018, landed as [!43](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/43), makes the war AI [choose, join, end and fight wars like a strategist](research/ai-strategy/prototypes.md). Seven switches in `AI_CUNNING_FLAGS` act on separate decisions. The AI declares once its army stands near the target. It compares the power each side can bring to the target's land, and reviews each war by its progress instead of a peace die. It pays only allies near the enemy to join its wars. Its stacks bombard with any unit that can, and a city's archers shoot. Over 16 Marathon games, 54 % of wars take a city instead of 40 %. Net cities per war rise from 0.75 to 1.08, and wars stalled for 50 turns fall from 3.4 to 1.5 per game; late turns are 7 % faster. What takes a city is first the stack's size, then how it is used: a collateral unit opening the attack, defenders worn down, archers on the walls rather than in the field stack. Bought wars were a quarter of all wars and took a city 8 % of the time. Before that: [a cunning opponent, measured](research/ai-strategy/README.md). Six Marathon games, 104 wars and 4,594 battles, compared with what a capable strategist would do. Once its army is in place the AI fights reasonably: in the wars where it takes a city, the first falls a median 7.5 turns after the declaration. Its weaknesses are when and where it commits. It declares war when the first group crosses the border, and in a fifth of its wars none of its army is near. 31 % of its wars are against stronger teams, and those lose cities on balance. Dogpiles take a city 24 % of the time. Peace is examined only when a die allows, so 18 wars ran 50+ turns with no city changing hands. 44 % of attacks are by a lone unit, and the exchange ratio is about 1:1. The report ranks seven changes that keep personality and chance deciding between good options. Patch 0017, landed as [!42](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/42), lets a civilization without cities found its capital: Genghis Khan never had one on Earth 12. It also stops needless HUD redraws and a 21 MB hover allocation, and cuts late turns by 8 % with identical games. Earlier research: [AI behaviour over four whole Marathon games](research/ai-behavior/README.md) (one civilization never founds a city on Earth 12, settlers wait for escorts, transports and generals sit idle) and [how the game runs](research/engine/README.md) (rendering and HUD requests, turn time, memory). Patch 0016, landed as [!41](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/41), fixes a random crash: an AI that razed a captured city read the city's name after deleting it. A Marathon game in the harness crashed on it at turn 415 and plays on with the fix. Before that, the work studied the AI in diplomacy and the late game: patches 0014 and 0015, both landed ([!39](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/39), [!40](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/40)). Mapping the deal, attitude and personality code found 14 defects. Examples: 32-bit overflow in the peace and surrender valuations, a received resource valued at a fifth of its worth, AI-to-AI resource swaps with no value check, tribute thresholds read from the wrong leader, and attitudes that lagged a turn. Fixed, the AIs fight fewer wars, at better odds, and win more of them. The headless harness first had to be corrected in three ways. It now applies RoM's Python building rules. It no longer leaks memory per callback: that leak, not slow DLL code, caused the Marathon crashes around turn 491. And its telemetry no longer changes the games it observes. A game reloaded from a save used to play differently from the running game; with 0014 and 0015 it plays identically. Patch 0015 also cuts late-game turn time by 17 % with an identical game. Before that, patch 0013 followed up on the same save: why Rome had no Writing at 410 BC, and what the settler overproduction was. Rome kept the Mercenaries civic for 125 turns because the AI valued its free experience at half of Rome's commerce, counted its unit charges at twice the real gold and could not disband any unit trained under it; unit charges took 45 of 96 commerce and research ran at 3 beakers a turn. The settlers were idle rather than overbuilt (141 created over the game, 122 cities founded), for causes patch 0012 had fixed. Patch 0013 has landed as [!38](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/38). Patch 0012 answers an autorun report as Masinissa (Marathon, 410 BC): the turn that never ended, slow turns, idle settlers and AIs that hoarded gold and trained Spearmen instead of buildings. The reported save was loaded into the real DLL in the headless harness and played on with every seat on AI. The endless turn came from a unit that lost its group on every update slice; turns now take 2.6 s instead of 9.6 s; settlers count only sites they can be delivered to and are disbanded when none remain; over 30 turns the AIs research 21 % more and complete 347 buildings instead of 238. Patch 0012 targets GitLab `main` after patch 0011 and has landed as [!37](https://gitlab.com/aretemaxxing-group/rise-of-mankind/-/merge_requests/37). Patch 0011 fixes settlers that never landed: at sea, no land unit aboard counted as an escort. Patch 0010 keeps AI path searches reusable, indexes escort grouping, and fixes siege positioning, synchronized landings, worker targeting and settlers abandoning a reached site. Patch 0009 fixes defects found by compiling the whole DLL, removes two turn-time hot spots and plans research as a minimum-cost prerequisite closure. Each ships an MSVC 2003-built DLL with provenance. Historical patches and assessments retain their original source identity.

The reviewed revision contains 252 Python files, 956 XML files and 277 native source/header files. The review combines whole-tree structural analysis with focused behavioral inspection. The original regression baseline had 12 passing programs and 2 failing programs; the first correctness patch passed all 15 programs. The current upstream and cleanup revisions pass 16 programs; the autoplay fixes pass 17, and the opening/transport changes pass 19 and the settlement changes pass 20. The BTS executable and a complete headless simulator were not run.

**The bundled DLL is older than the source**, as confirmed by the repository owner. These reports assess the pinned source. In-game comparisons require a rebuilt DLL with recorded source/build provenance; the existing binary is not an oracle for the reviewed code. See [the provenance record](docs/review/source-binary-provenance.json). `builds/` now holds such rebuilt DLLs for `d8cedd5` and patch 0009.

The new Carthage autoplay report concerns a DLL the user compiled locally from current GitLab source, not that older bundled binary. Its save metadata and provenance limits are documented separately.

The reports include fixed-revision source links, a state-transition model, deterministic replay requirements, engine boundaries and proposed tests. The machine-readable inventories and review scripts are in `docs/review/`.

The deeper review reproduces five source-level issues and verifies two lifecycle/identity contracts. `simulation/` contains validated schema examples and a contract validator; it does not yet implement a game simulator. Run the commands in its README to validate the examples and reproduce the new source observations.
