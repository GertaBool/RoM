## Patriarch

Authority gathers around the head of a household, whose kinship ties bind families into a tribe. Custom and personal loyalty carry decisions farther than written law.

**Strategy:** No civic upkeep, but distance and city-count maintenance make expansion costly.

## Oligarchy

Behind the council doors, a few influential families decide the affairs of many. Wealth, lineage or military power secures a voice that most inhabitants never receive.

**Strategy:** Supports experienced recruits and a happier palace city; allow for higher distance maintenance.

## Priesthood

The sanctuary becomes a seat of government as well as worship. Priests interpret sacred obligations, preserve records and lend religious authority to judgments and public decisions.

**Strategy:** Favors priest specialists and capital research and culture; a large empire pays more city-count maintenance.

## Monarchy

A crown passes through a dynasty, joining the fortunes of a realm to those of a family. Succession promises continuity, yet disputed inheritances can turn the royal household into a battlefield.

**Strategy:** Favors military production and noble specialists; balance these benefits against higher distance maintenance.

## Assembly

Public affairs enter the meeting place, where eligible citizens debate and vote. The reach of that citizenship determines whose consent matters and whose voice remains outside the gathering.

**Strategy:** Favors Great People and faster workers, but slows military production and raises several maintenance costs.

## Patrician

Old family names open the doors of the senate and the counting house. In Rome and later urban republics, patrician elites joined inherited prestige to influence over public office and commerce.

## Imperium

Orders leave the capital under the seal of supreme command. Governors, magistrates and soldiers carry imperial authority across provinces whose languages and customs may differ greatly.

**Strategy:** Useful for military production and infrastructure work; budget for higher distance and city-count maintenance.

## Fraternity

Oaths of fellowship bind associations, lodges and civic circles into networks of mutual aid. Their shared loyalties can support public cooperation while drawing a sharp boundary around membership.

**Strategy:** Favors specialists, Great People and espionage; watch maintenance costs and increased war weariness.

## Technocracy

Engineers and specialists bring the language of measurement into government. Technical knowledge informs public decisions, but expertise alone cannot settle whose interests those decisions should serve.

**Strategy:** Favors production, specialists and espionage; its high civic upkeep requires a strong treasury.

## Oral Law

Law lives in remembered judgments, public testimony and the words of respected elders. Its authority depends on shared custom and the people trusted to preserve it.

**Strategy:** A starting arrangement with substantial yield and worker-speed penalties; improved record keeping deserves early consideration.

## Tablets

A stylus presses obligations into clay: grain received, workers supplied, debts unpaid. Records outlast their witnesses, although local signs and measures can still leave distant officials puzzled.

**Strategy:** Reduces the yield penalties of Oral Law and eases distance maintenance, but still limits economic output.

## Standardized Tablets

Common measures and familiar forms make one storehouse intelligible to another. A record can now travel with its meaning intact, extending administration beyond personal acquaintance.

**Strategy:** Removes the yield penalties of basic tablets and further reduces distance maintenance; city-count maintenance remains higher.

## Accounting

Receipts and obligations become quantities that can be compared. Officials use accounts to follow resources through the state, revealing shortfalls that memory or ceremony might conceal.

**Strategy:** Favors gold-producing cities and distant holdings; compare the savings with higher civic and city-count upkeep.

## Bookkeeping

Daybooks and ledgers gather scattered transactions into a continuous record. The clerk can trace yesterday’s promise into tomorrow’s payment, making complex obligations easier to administer and inspect.

**Strategy:** Useful for gold, espionage and distant cities; its administrative costs still rise with the size of the empire.

## Double-Entry Bookkeeping

Every transaction leaves two corresponding entries in the books. By balancing debits and credits, merchants and treasuries gain a powerful check on errors, though balanced accounts alone cannot guarantee honesty.

## Digital Administration

Files become searchable records moving through electronic networks. Automation can shorten routine administration, while the reach of shared databases makes accuracy, access and security matters of public importance.

## No Taxes

Households retain what they produce without a regular public levy. Rulers must look to other resources or voluntary contributions to sustain the institutions and undertakings they wish to maintain.

**Strategy:** A no-upkeep fiscal option; compare it with the economic benefits and costs of more developed systems.

## Tribute

Goods and labor flow toward a dominant court as signs of submission. The arrangement can bind distant communities to a ruler, but its burdens are seldom shared equally.

**Strategy:** Concentrates benefits in the capital, while reducing food output and increasing maintenance and war weariness.

## Census

The state counts households, property and obligations, giving officials a map of resources they might otherwise overlook. To be recorded is to become more visible to both public services and public demands.

**Strategy:** Favors gold and capital commerce; account for higher city-count maintenance and war weariness.

## Fiscus

Taxes pass through appointed collectors into an organized treasury. Regular accounts and dedicated officials give the state a steadier financial machinery, bringing its demands more firmly into everyday life.

## National Debt

The treasury borrows against revenues still to come. Credit can finance an emergency or a lasting investment, but repayment ties future budgets to decisions made today.

## National Banking

Banks connect savers, borrowers and public finance across a national economy. Their promises circulate on trust, making confidence in institutions as important as the coins held in their vaults.

## Planned Allocation

Budgets assign resources according to priorities set at the center. Planners seek to coordinate competing demands, relying on reports from distant workplaces to turn national targets into practical deliveries.

## Hunter-Gatherer

Seasonal paths connect fishing grounds, hunting places and edible plants. Detailed knowledge of local landscapes sustains communities whose ways of life vary with their environments.

**Strategy:** No civic upkeep, but slower workers and military production make development and mobilization harder.

## Agricultural

Fields and granaries anchor the rhythm of the year. Cultivation can support settled communities and food reserves, while binding livelihoods to land, water and the uncertain harvest.

**Strategy:** Best where farms and related agricultural improvements are worked; the commerce gain comes with slower Great People generation.

## Pastoral

Herds turn grasslands into milk, meat, wool and wealth. Pastoral communities organize movement and exchange around the needs of their animals and the changing availability of pasture.

**Strategy:** Favors worked pastures with extra food and commerce; strongest where livestock resources are plentiful.

## Coastal

Nets, tides and sheltered anchorages shape the working day. Fishing communities look seaward for food and exchange, linking their fortunes to waters that can sustain or endanger them.

**Strategy:** Favors worked fishing-boat improvements with production and commerce; unimproved sea tiles receive neither bonus.

## Barter

Goods and services change hands without money mediating the exchange. Barter can coexist with gifts, credit and currency, depending on what partners need and what they trust.

**Strategy:** Cheaper Markets help develop the economy; prolonged wars bring greater war weariness.

## Precious Metals

Weighed silver and gold carry value beyond a single market. Scales, agreed standards and tests of purity make precious metal a useful means of payment and a store of wealth.

**Strategy:** Compare its low upkeep and listed effects with available alternatives; it carries increased war weariness.

## Coinage

A stamped piece of metal carries a promise of weight and worth. Coins simplify many transactions, while the issuing authority must defend the confidence placed in its mark.

**Strategy:** Adds trade-route capacity; connect cities to profitable partners and weigh the extra income against civic upkeep.

## Usury

Lending for interest places a price on the use of money over time. Laws and religious traditions have drawn different boundaries between legitimate credit and exploitative debt.

**Strategy:** Raises gold and trade capacity, but harms health, happiness, worker speed and Great People generation.

## Colonial

Imperial trade binds distant territories to the needs of a ruling center. Settlers, monopolies and coercive labor systems can redirect land and wealth at the expense of colonized peoples.

**Strategy:** Useful for dispersed empires: reduces distance and city-count maintenance while adding trade-route capacity.

## Protectionism

Tariffs and restrictions shelter domestic producers from foreign competition. The policy can protect livelihoods and strategic industries, while raising costs and inviting retaliation from trading partners.

**Strategy:** Favors gold, merchant specialists and trade capacity; judge its value against your actual city incomes.

## Corporate

Large companies gather capital, labor and influence across many markets. Public policy can support their expansion, creating powerful productive networks alongside difficult questions of competition and accountability.

**Strategy:** Favors production, gold and corporations, but substantial health and large-city happiness penalties can constrain growth.

## Planned

Production follows targets set through administrative plans rather than market prices alone. Coordinating factories and farms on this scale makes reliable information and timely supplies constant concerns.

**Strategy:** Favors rapid worker improvements and espionage, but reduces gold output and excludes corporations.

## National Socialism

National Socialism fused dictatorship, racial hierarchy and expansionist war. In Nazi Germany, state persecution, forced labor and genocide were central to a regime that subordinated institutions to its ideology.

**Strategy:** Foreign trade routes are forbidden under this civic; account for the loss of trading partners before adopting it.

## Regulated

Public spending and taxation are used to influence demand across the economic cycle. The aim is to soften unemployment and instability, though timing, debt and inflation complicate every intervention.

**Strategy:** Favors production, gold, engineer specialists and trade capacity; strongest where cities can use those advantages.

## Aboriginal Lifestyle

Work is organized through local knowledge, kinship and customary obligations. Indigenous societies encompass many different traditions; no single pattern of labor or ownership describes them all.

**Strategy:** A no-upkeep starting option, but workers act more slowly and corporations are unavailable.

## Slavery

Enslaved people are denied freedom and compelled to work under another’s control. Wealth drawn from their labor rests on coercion, violence and the disruption of families and communities.

**Strategy:** Raises production, gold and selected improvement yields; balance the gain against high upkeep, distance maintenance and war weariness.

## Caste

Inherited social categories shape occupation, marriage and access to privilege. Their rules vary across places and periods, but the authority of birth can make exclusion persist across generations.

**Strategy:** Favors health, military production and selected improvements; slower workers make infrastructure projects take longer.

## Proletariat

Wage earners claim a collective voice in the society their labor sustains. Movements speaking for the working class have pursued different forms of representation, ownership and political power.

**Strategy:** Useful for faster workers and improved farm, mine and workshop yields; gains depend on the tiles your cities work.

## Qualified Labor

Apprenticeship, study and practice turn experience into recognized skill. Specialized workers can undertake demanding tasks, while training and access to qualifications shape who can enter their trades.

**Strategy:** Favors rapid improvements and strong tile yields across an established empire; match your workforce to the available land.

## Labor Allocation

Administrative offices assign workers according to planned needs. The promise of full employment comes with decisions over occupation and wages that may leave individuals little room to choose.

## Homeschooling

The household is the first classroom, where children learn through instruction, imitation and daily work. What each family can pass on depends on its knowledge, resources and time.

**Strategy:** No civic upkeep; compare its limited economic benefits with the stronger education options available later.

## Elders

Experienced members of the community teach skills, stories and obligations to the next generation. Their authority gives learning continuity, while tying it closely to inherited custom.

**Strategy:** Favors commerce and Great People without civic upkeep; an early option for developing the economy.

## Tutors

A dedicated teacher guides a small circle of pupils through lessons suited to their needs. Personal attention can deepen learning, but access often follows wealth and social standing.

**Strategy:** Improves food, production and commerce while encouraging Great People; useful across varied city specializations.

## Academies

Teachers and students gather around a lasting institution rather than a single household. Libraries, debate and shared methods give scholarship a home that can survive its founders.

**Strategy:** Combines stronger yields with health and experience for new units; useful when growth and recruitment both matter.

## Trade School

The workshop joins the classroom as a place of deliberate instruction. Practical training prepares skilled workers to turn plans and materials into the structures and machinery of everyday life.

**Strategy:** Favors production, faster workers and engineer specialists; useful for building infrastructure and industrial cities.

## Humanistic

Literature, history and critical inquiry invite students to examine the human world. Education cultivates judgment and expression as well as useful knowledge, opening old authorities to renewed debate.

**Strategy:** Favors research, culture and Great People, with unrestricted scientist and artist assignments; provide food to support those specialists.

## Public Schools

Public funds bring education beyond the households that can purchase it privately. Shared curricula can widen opportunity and build common knowledge, while making the purposes of schooling a public question.

**Strategy:** Favors production and free specialists, but imposes a substantial health penalty and high civic upkeep.

## Folklore

Stories, festivals and customary rites carry shared meanings through generations. Religious and everyday traditions mingle in practices that give familiar places and seasonal events a remembered significance.

**Strategy:** Provides experience for new units and reduces war weariness without civic upkeep.

## Spiritism

Ritual specialists seek guidance from spirits, ancestors or unseen powers. In this civic, the name covers such varied practices rather than a single historical movement or creed.

**Strategy:** Favors Great People and espionage; its building-specific happiness bonuses reward the appropriate wonders.

## Divine Cult

The ruler’s person becomes an object of sacred honor. Temples, ceremonies and public images join political obedience to religious duty, lending the throne authority beyond ordinary office.

**Strategy:** Favors the capital and construction in state-religion cities, but sharply reduces Great People generation and carries high upkeep.

## Sacrifice Cult

Offerings express obligations toward sacred powers and the community. Their forms have ranged from goods and animals to human victims, with ritual authority sometimes enforcing devastating demands on the vulnerable.

**Strategy:** Allows population-based production rushing; weigh immediate output against population loss and happiness penalties.

## Fanaticism

Absolute conviction leaves little room for compromise or dissent. Institutions mobilize loyalty around a single cause, and those outside its boundaries may face exclusion, coercion or persecution.

**Strategy:** Favors military recruitment in state-religion cities; other religions bring unhappiness and corporations are excluded.

## Pantheon

Many gods share the sacred landscape, each associated with particular places, powers or occupations. Civic festivals and household worship connect public life to a varied community of divine patrons.

**Strategy:** Favors commerce, culture and religious happiness; Hellenic monasteries receive additional health and happiness benefits.

## State Religion

One faith receives formal recognition and support from the state. Its institutions can bind public life together, while the position of other believers depends on the limits of official toleration.

**Strategy:** Most valuable when your state religion is widespread: it supports happiness, recruitment and construction in those cities.

## Occult

Hidden correspondences and guarded teachings promise knowledge beyond ordinary appearances. Esoteric circles pursue initiation and interpretation, often standing apart from established religious institutions.

**Strategy:** Favors research, espionage and specialists; its unrestricted scientist and artist assignments support specialized cities.

## Scientism

Scientific explanation is treated as the chief authority for understanding society and the world. Unlike scientific inquiry itself, scientism extends that authority into questions of value and purpose.

**Strategy:** Favors research and production while reducing corporation maintenance; supports scientist-focused cities.

## Tribal Warfare

Warriors gather through kinship and personal allegiance when danger or opportunity calls. Command rests on reputation and social ties rather than a permanent military establishment.

**Strategy:** A no-upkeep starting military organization; later options offer stronger training, support or mobilization tools.

## Raiders

Speed and surprise carry armed bands beyond the frontier. Raids seek movable wealth and captives, enriching attackers while leaving threatened communities to bear destruction and insecurity.

**Strategy:** Favors experienced recruits, unit support and Great Generals; useful for maintaining an active early army.

## Fortification

Walls, watch posts and guarded gates turn settlement into a defensive system. Their protection depends as much on supplies and vigilant defenders as on the strength of stone.

**Strategy:** Favors defended cities: garrisons, walls and castles improve happiness, while fighting at home accelerates Great General progress.

## Mercenaries

Military service becomes a contract between employer and armed specialists. Experienced soldiers can be hired beyond local obligations, but their continued service depends on payment and the terms of employment.

**Strategy:** Adds experience to new units, but increases both general and military unit costs; budget for the army you actually maintain.

## Standing Army

Troops remain organized and supplied after the campaign ends. Permanent units preserve training and command, while their presence makes military expenditure a continuing responsibility of government.

**Strategy:** Favors experienced recruits and garrison happiness; its military unit surcharge makes army size a continuing expense.

## Militia

Citizens combine ordinary livelihoods with an obligation to muster under arms. Local participation can broaden defense, though training, equipment and readiness vary with the demands of civilian life.

**Strategy:** Population increases the free-unit allowance, while garrisons provide happiness; useful for supporting a broad defensive force.

## Professional Army

Soldiering becomes a sustained career supported by specialist training and established command. Expertise accumulates through service, demanding reliable recruitment, supplies and pay to preserve it.

**Strategy:** Favors highly experienced recruits and strong garrison happiness; high civic upkeep and military costs demand reliable income.

## Conscription

The state calls eligible inhabitants into military service by law. Conscription can mobilize forces on a broad scale, while drawing people away from households, workplaces and their own plans.

**Strategy:** Enables drafting, but increases war weariness and makes barracks less popular; weigh emergency recruitment against city growth and happiness.
