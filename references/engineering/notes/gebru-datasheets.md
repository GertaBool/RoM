# Timnit Gebru, Jamie Morgenstern, Briana Vecchione, Jennifer Wortman Vaughan, Hanna Wallach, Hal Daumé III, Kate Crawford, Datasheets for Datasets (arXiv:1803.09010v8 [cs.DB], 1 Dec 2021)
Source: lit3/gebru-datasheets/doc.txt. Read: lines 1-1002 (all, including references and the appendix example datasheet).

## What it says
- p. 1 (1 Introduction): "The characteristics of these datasets fundamentally influence a model’s behavior" -- a model is unlikely to perform well if its deployment context does not match its training or evaluation datasets, or if they reflect unwanted societal biases.
- p. 1 (1 Introduction): "machine learning models can reproduce or amplify unwanted societal biases reflected in training datasets" -- mismatches are especially severe in high-stakes domains (criminal justice, hiring, critical infrastructure, finance).
- p. 1 (1 Introduction): "there is currently no standardized process for documenting machine learning datasets." -- data provenance is studied in databases but rarely discussed in ML.
- p. 1 (1 Introduction): "In the electronics industry, every component, no matter how simple or complex, is accompanied with a datasheet" -- the analogy behind the proposal.
- p. 2 (1 Introduction): "dataset be accompanied with a datasheet that documents its motivation, composition, collection process, recommended uses, and so on." -- with the stated potential to increase transparency and accountability, mitigate biases, "facilitate greater reproducibility of machine learning results", and help select datasets.
- p. 2 (1.1 Objectives): "For dataset creators, the primary objective is to encourage careful reflection on the process of creating, distributing, and maintaining a dataset" -- including assumptions, risks and implications of use.
- p. 2 (1.1 Objectives): "For dataset consumers, the primary objective is to ensure they have the information they need to make informed decisions about using a dataset."
- p. 2 (1.1 Objectives): "researchers and practitioners without access to a dataset may be able to use the information in its datasheet to create alternative datasets with similar characteristics." -- the secondary objective of reproducibility.
- p. 2 (1.1 Objectives): "these questions are not intended to be prescriptive." -- datasheets will vary with domain and organizational workflows.
- p. 2 (footnote 1): "in some cases, the people creating a datasheet for a dataset may not be the" -- dataset creators, as with the authors' own example datasheets.
- p. 3 (1.1 Objectives): "We emphasize that the process of creating a datasheet is not intended to be automated." -- automated documentation runs counter to the objective of careful reflection.
- p. 3 (2 Development Process): "We refined the questions and workflow provided in the next section over a period of roughly two years, incorporating many rounds of feedback."
- p. 3 (2 Development Process): the questions were tested by writing example datasheets for Labeled Faces in the Wild and Pang and Lee's polarity dataset, which revealed "gaps in the questions, as well as redundancies and lack of clarity."
- p. 3 (2 Development Process): the refined questions went to product teams in two major US technology companies, with the authors "observing where the questions did not achieve their intended objectives." -- plus feedback from dozens of researchers via arXiv and social media, and a legal review.
- p. 3 (2 Development Process): "we reworded the questions to discourage yes/no answers" -- they also added a section on Uses and deleted a section on Legal and Ethical Considerations.
- p. 3 (2 Development Process): "product teams were more likely to answer questions about legal and ethical considerations if they were integrated into sections about the relevant stages of the dataset lifecycle rather than grouped together."
- pp. 3-4 (2 Development Process): after the lawyers' feedback they removed questions on regulatory compliance and "introduced factual questions intended to" elicit compliance information "without requiring dataset creators to make legal judgments."
- p. 4 (3 Questions and Workflow): sections "roughly match the key stages of the dataset lifecycle: motivation, composition, collection process, preprocessing/cleaning/labeling, uses, distribution, and maintenance."
- p. 4 (3 Questions and Workflow): the grouping encourages creators to reflect "and even alter this process in response to their reflection." -- questions that do not apply should be skipped.
- p. 4 (3 Questions and Workflow): some example answers are "Unknown to the authors of the datasheet." because the authors did not create the dataset and could not find the answer in its documentation.
- p. 4 (3 Questions and Workflow): "We recommend answering as many questions as possible rather than skipping the datasheet creation process entirely." -- even creators may not be able to answer all.
- p. 4 (3.1 Motivation): questions to "clearly articulate their reasons for creating the dataset and to promote transparency about funding interests." -- e.g. "For what purpose was the dataset created? Was there a specific task in mind?" and who created and funded it.
- p. 5 (3.2 Composition): "Dataset creators should read through these questions prior to any data collection and then provide answers once data collection is complete."
- p. 5 (3.2 Composition): questions include what instances represent, how many, and "Does the dataset contain all possible instances or is it a sample" -- and if a sample, whether and how representativeness was validated.
- p. 5 (3.2 Composition): "Is any information missing from individual instances? If so, please provide a description, explaining why this information is missing" -- also labels, relationships between instances, and recommended data splits with their rationale.
- p. 6 (3.2 Composition): "Are there any errors, sources of noise, or redundancies in the dataset?"
- p. 6 (3.2 Composition): "Is the dataset self-contained, or does it link to or otherwise rely on external resources" -- with whether they are guaranteed to "exist, and remain constant, over time" and whether official archival versions exist.
- p. 6 (3.2 Composition): further questions on confidential, offensive or sensitive data, subpopulations, and whether individuals can be identified; the people-related ones may be skipped "If the dataset does not relate to people".
- p. 6 (3.3 Collection Process): these questions also aim "to elicit information that may help researchers and practitioners to create alternative datasets with similar characteristics."
- p. 7 (3.3 Collection Process): "Was the data directly observable (e.g., raw text, movie ratings), reported by subjects (e.g., survey responses), or indirectly inferred/derived from other data" -- and whether derived data was validated.
- p. 7 (3.3 Collection Process): "What mechanisms or procedures were used to collect the data" and "How were these mechanisms or procedures validated?" -- plus sampling strategy, who collected it and how paid, timeframe, and ethical review.
- p. 7 (3.3 Collection Process): "Does this timeframe match the creation timeframe of the data associated with the instances" -- e.g. a recent crawl of old news articles.
- pp. 7-8 (3.3 Collection Process): for data about people: collected directly or via third parties, notification, consent with "the exact language to which the individuals consented", revocation, and impact analysis.
- p. 8 (3.4 Preprocessing/cleaning/labeling): the aim is to let consumers determine whether the raw data "has been processed in ways that are compatible with their chosen tasks." -- e.g. a bag-of-words is not suitable for tasks involving word order.
- p. 8 (3.4 Preprocessing/cleaning/labeling): whether the raw data was saved "in addition to the preprocessed/cleaned/labeled data" and "Is the software that was used to preprocess/clean/label the data available?"
- p. 8 (3.5 Uses): "By explicitly highlighting these tasks, dataset creators can help dataset consumers to make informed decisions" -- questions on prior uses, a repository of papers using it, and other tasks.
- p. 9 (3.5 Uses): "Is there anything about the composition of the dataset or the way it was collected and preprocessed/cleaned/labeled that might impact future uses?" and "Are there tasks for which the dataset should not be used?"
- p. 9 (3.6 Distribution): questions on distribution to third parties, method and DOI, timing, license or terms of use, third-party IP restrictions and export controls, answered "prior to distributing the dataset".
- p. 10 (3.7 Maintenance): "Is there an erratum? If so, please provide a link or other access point." -- with who maintains it, how to contact them, and "Will the dataset be updated" (how often, by whom, how communicated).
- p. 10 (3.7 Maintenance): "Will older versions of the dataset continue to be supported/hosted/maintained?" -- if not, how obsolescence will be communicated; and whether "these contributions be validated/verified" when others extend the dataset.
- p. 10 (4 Impact and Challenges): adoption since March 2018: academic datasets with datasheets, pilots at Microsoft, Google and IBM, model cards, a data card for Open Images, IBM factsheets, the Dataset Nutrition Label, and Partnership on AI guidance.
- p. 11 (4 Impact and Challenges): "Chief among them is the need for dataset creators to modify the questions and workflow provided in the previous section based on their existing organizational infrastructure and workflows."
- p. 11 (4 Impact and Challenges): "the questions and workflow may pose problems for dynamic datasets. If a dataset changes only infrequently, we recommend accompanying updated versions with updated datasheets."
- p. 11 (4 Impact and Challenges): "Datasheets for datasets do not provide a complete solution to mitigating unwanted societal biases or potential risks or harms." -- creators cannot anticipate every use, and demographic labels may be unavailable.
- p. 11 (4 Impact and Challenges): for data about people it "may be necessary for dataset creators to work with experts in other domains such as anthropology, sociology, and science and technology studies."
- p. 11 (4 Impact and Challenges): "creating datasheets for datasets will necessarily impose overhead on dataset creators." -- they may save time on one-off questions, but take time, and workflows and incentives must change.
- p. 11 (4 Impact and Challenges): "Ultimately, we believe that the benefits to the machine learning community outweigh the costs."
- p. 15 (Appendix, example datasheet): footnote: "All information in this datasheet is taken from one of the following five sources; any errors that were introduced are the fault of the authors of the datasheet" -- the example names its sources.
- p. 15 (Appendix, Composition): "There are 1,400 instances in total in the original (v1.x versions) and 2,000 instances in total in v2.0 (from 2014)." -- the sample is intended to be random with at most 40 posts per author, and "No tests were run to determine representativeness."
- p. 15 (Appendix, Composition): "Some additional unspecified automatic filtering was done." -- recorded as part of what each instance consists of.
- p. 16 (Appendix, Composition and Collection Process): several answers read "Unknown to the authors of the datasheet." -- e.g. confidentiality, collection mechanisms, timeframe, and ethical review.
- p. 17 (Appendix, Preprocessing): the labeling rule is stated exactly (five-star scale: at least four positive, at most two negative; four-star scale: three and up positive, one or less negative) and "Occasionally half stars are missed which affects the labeling of negative examples."
- p. 17 (Appendix, Preprocessing): "Some preprocessing errors were caught in later versions." -- the fixes are listed, and the preprocessing software is not available.
- p. 17 (Appendix, Uses): "systems trained on it may or may not generalize to other sentiment prediction tasks." -- so such systems should not without additional verification be used for consequential decisions about people.
- p. 17 (Appendix, Distribution): "The dataset does not have a DOI and there is no redundant archive."
- p. 18 (Appendix, Maintenance): "There is not an explicit erratum, but updates and known errors are specified in higher version README and diff files." -- and "older versions are kept around for consistency."

## Readings for our work
- Give each dataset we derive from game saves and logs (one per game or per harness batch) a short datasheet in the repository answering: purpose, which DLL build and XML version produced it, scenario and options, players and starts, how many turns and runs, what was dropped or filtered and why, known errors, and which tool version transformed it. Rests on: motivation, composition, collection and preprocessing questions (pp. 4-8), the example's stated filtering and labeling rules (pp. 15-17). Would not apply if: the data are regenerated from a fully specified command in the repo on every use.
- Mark unknowns in the datasheet as unknown rather than guessing, matching our rule to mark unknowns open. Rests on: answers given as Unknown to the authors of the datasheet (p. 4, p. 16), answer as many questions as possible (p. 4). Would not apply if: the field can be read from the save or log, in which case read it.
- Do not generate the datasheet automatically from the checks; it is meant to make the author reflect on how the data was made and what it should not be used for. Automation is fine for factual fields (build hash, row counts) but the uses and limits sections must be written. Rests on: not intended to be automated (p. 3). Would not apply if: the datasheet is used only as a machine-readable manifest, which is a different purpose.
- Record for each harness dataset the tasks it should not be used for, e.g. results under one map script or one opponent set do not stand for other settings without verification. Rests on: tasks for which the dataset should not be used (p. 9), the example's generalisation limit (p. 17). Would not apply if: the experiment already spans all settings it claims.
- When a dataset is corrected (a logging bug fixed, a run discarded), bump its version, keep the old one, and record the change in an erratum file. Rests on: erratum and versions questions (p. 10), the example's README and diff files (p. 18), updated versions with updated datasheets (p. 11). Would not apply if: the data are never shared or reused beyond one analysis.
- Keep the template short and adapted to our setting; the authors state the questions are not prescriptive and impose overhead, so the template should be pruned to fields we actually use. Rests on: not prescriptive (p. 2), overhead (p. 11), modify to workflows (p. 11). Would not apply if: a field turns out to prevent a recurring error.
