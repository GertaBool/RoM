## Readings for our work
- Reading (inference): do not use our own commit messages as the record of what a commit changed; classify a commit by its diff. A check that wants to know whether a patch to the DLL is pure restructuring should compare the trees (for example, compiled output or a normalised diff), not grep the message. Rests on: p. 6 (63 vs 112 refactorings), p. 5 (refactoring may be unconscious or subordinate), p. 10 (bug titles in messages caused misclassification). Would not apply if: our gate mechanically writes the message from the diff, so that message and content cannot diverge.
- Reading (inference): mixed commits are the normal case (floss refactoring), so a rule that each commit carries one concern will be broken unless it is enforced mechanically; a pre-commit check can flag commits that touch both restructuring and behaviour (for example, a whitespace or line-ending only file next to a logic change). Rests on: p. 7 (floss 30% vs root-canal 3% of commits; 98% of refactorings in floss commits), p. 1 (interspersing confirmed). Would not apply if: the separation is already done by tooling that splits commits, or if our work has no restructuring at all.
- Reading (inference): the Pure Whitespace category shows that whitespace-only changes are a distinct and detectable kind of change; a line-ending rewrite in a CRLF code base can be caught by comparing the diff with and without whitespace normalisation and refusing a patch where they differ in size by more than the intended lines. Rests on: p. 5-6 (Table 3 categories: Pure Whitespace counted separately). Would not apply if: the target repository normalises line endings on checkout and commit itself.
- Reading (inference): self-report overestimates tool use; claims in our documents about how often we did something (ran a check, used a script) should come from logs, not from recollection. Rests on: p. 8 (survey median 68% vs 11% measured), p. 8 (refactoring "often not a conscious activity"). Would not apply if: the claim is backed by a recorded log already.
- Reading (inference): a detector sees only what it was built to see; any automatic check we use (for example, a diff classifier or a quote checker) should state which classes of change it cannot detect, and its miss rate should be measured once against a manual blind sample. Rests on: p. 7 (high-level-only detectors miss 40 to 60 percent), p. 10 (be aware of what level the tool can detect), p. 5 (blind manual comparison as ground truth). Would not apply if: the check is exhaustive by construction (for example, a byte comparison).
- Reading (inference): when a check is calibrated by its own author, a blind comparison guards against bias in the same hand; for a check on our own analyses, the labeller should not know which outcome the check expects. Rests on: p. 6 (blind comparison applies bias equally to both sets). Would not apply if: the check has no judgement step.
- Reading (inference): results from one project and one environment carry limits; findings drawn from one game, one map or one seed set should be stated with that scope and replicated before they guide an AI change. Rests on: p. 1 (single case studies may not hold), p. 9 (repeat in other projects), p. 10 (Java and Eclipse only). Would not apply if: the change is evaluated over the full distribution it will face.
